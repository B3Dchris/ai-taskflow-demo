The problem

Class shows you moves. Nobody shows you the map.

You learn an armbar on Tuesday and it's gone by Thursday. You know five techniques but not when to use which, or what to do when the first one fails. Most of jiu-jitsu happens between the moves: what they do, and what you switch to.

JitsFlow puts the whole picture in your pocket, so the time between classes counts.

How it works

Ten minutes off the mat, twice a week.

  1. Rate what you know

    Five minutes, once. JitsFlow sees your gaps and builds your weekly plan around your class days.

  2. Study before class

    Review a few cards, read the steps, watch the video, trace the chain. Walk in knowing what you're working on.

  3. Log after class

    What was taught, what worked, what tapped you, where you got stuck. JitsFlow points you to what to study next.

Does it replace my coach?

No. JitsFlow is for study between classes. Coaches teach details differently, so moves where that matters are flagged with "Check with your coach", and each belt has a coach review list to go through together.

What does it cost?

It's free to use, with no account needed.

Where's my data?

On your phone. Nothing is uploaded. Settings › Backup copies everything so you can move it to another device.

Does it work at the gym with no signal?

Yes. Once opened, JitsFlow works offline. Videos need a connection.

How do I put it on my home screen?

iPhone: open jitsflow.com in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. Android: open it in Chrome and tap Install app. It opens full screen, like any app.

Who made the videos?

Instructors on YouTube. JitsFlow links each move to a clear instructional video and plays it from YouTube; the videos belong to their creators.

Is it safe to learn leg locks from an app?

Learn the basics here, but always learn finishes, especially heel hooks, from a coach in person. JitsFlow deliberately leaves out heel hook finishing directions. Tap early in training.
