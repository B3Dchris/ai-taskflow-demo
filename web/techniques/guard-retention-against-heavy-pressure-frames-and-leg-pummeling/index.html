Guard retention against heavy pressure: frames and leg pummeling

Keeping your guard against heavy passers with frames, hip movement and leg pummeling, without inverting. Use it when a pressure passer starts to pin your legs and flatten you.

Seated guard → Seated guard

Step by step

  1. Get on your hip facing them. Never lie flat. Put one forearm across their collarbone and the other hand on their hip or biceps, elbows tight.
  2. When they drive in, hip escape away from your frames to make space. Your frames hold the distance; your hips move.
  3. Pummel your near knee into the space between you as a shield.
  4. If they pin one leg, circle that foot inside and up to their hip or biceps to free it. Keep both feet active, one after the other.
  5. If they get past your knees, keep the frame on their neck, hip out, and bring your knee back to the middle. If that fails, get the underhook and come up to your knees rather than inverting.
  6. Once both knees are back in front of their hips with your feet connected to them, reset your guard.

Details that make it work

  • Frames stop the weight. Hip movement makes the space. You need both.
  • Recover with knees and feet, not by rolling under. Inversion is slow against heavy passers and loads your neck.

Common mistakes

  • Pushing with straight arms, so they collapse your frames and lie on you.
  • Waiting until they are fully past your hips to start working.

Drill it

Partner passes with pressure at 70 percent. 6 rounds of 1 minute. No inverting. Score a point each time you get both knees back in front.
