Back retention: re-seating hooks when they slide out

You keep the seatbelt and follow with your hips to put the hooks back in. Use it every time they slide down and clear a hook during a back escape.

Back control → Back control

Step by step

  1. Keep the seatbelt tight and your chest glued to their back.
  2. When they slide their hips down and push a hook off, move your hips first: follow them down so your chest stays on their back.
  3. Put the cleared foot on the mat or their hip and push their hips back in line with yours.
  4. Slide the hook back in on the inside of their thigh, toes turned out.
  5. If they keep clearing hooks, lock a body triangle around their waist.
  6. If their shoulders reach the mat on your top-arm side, stop chasing and climb to mount.

Details that make it work

  • Hips follow first, hooks second. A hook without your hips behind it comes straight back out.
  • The seatbelt matters more than the hooks. Never give it up to chase a hook.

Common mistakes

  • Crossing your ankles in front of them, which gets your foot locked.
  • Reaching for the hook with your foot while your chest drifts off their back.

Drill it

3 rounds of 2 minutes: partner works the back escape, you keep the seatbelt and re-seat hooks.

If it fails, or what comes next
