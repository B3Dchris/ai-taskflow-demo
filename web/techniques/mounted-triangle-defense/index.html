Mounted triangle defense

How to stop the triangle from mount: keep your arm off your face and bridge before they lock their feet. Use it when they climb high and isolate an arm.

Mounted → Mounted

Step by step

  1. Prevent it: elbows tight and hands high. The mounted triangle starts when one of your arms gets pulled across your face or above your head.
  2. If they isolate an arm and slide up toward your head, pull that elbow back to your ribs at once and bridge to slide them back down.
  3. If their shin gets behind your head, grab your trapped hand with your free hand. Keep the arm bent and tight; they'll look for the armbar too.
  4. Before they lock their feet, bridge hard into them and turn to your side so they can't square up over you.
  5. Once they're off balance, pull your trapped arm back in and work your elbow-knee escape.
  6. Tap once the triangle is locked and they pull your head or arm. Don't wait for the armbar.

Details that make it work

  • Win early, at the arm. The finished position is very hard to escape.
  • Keep the trapped arm bent, always.

Common mistakes

  • Pushing their hips with a straight arm, which feeds the arm across.
  • Lying flat while they adjust their legs.

Drill it

Partner in high mount works toward the mounted triangle at 50%: 5 reps each side of stopping the arm, then 2 × 1 min positional.

If it fails, or what comes next

  • Mount defense: protect your neck and arms Escape · Mounted
  • Elbow-knee escape (shrimp) Escape · Mounted
