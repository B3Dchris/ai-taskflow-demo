Snap-down to front headlock

You pull their head down sharply so they drop to their hands and knees in front of you. It's the most reliable no-gi takedown for a beginner and leads straight into chokes and back takes.

Standing → Front headlock

Step by step

  1. Get a collar tie (hand behind their neck) and control their other wrist.
  2. Snap their head down toward their own knees as you step back.
  3. As they drop to a knee or their hands, sprawl your hips back so your weight lands on their head and shoulders.
  4. Lock the front headlock: one arm around the head with your forearm under the chin, other hand on their far elbow or triceps.
  5. Keep your hips low and back; circle toward the side of the arm you control.

Details that make it work

  • The snap works best right after they lean or reach toward you.
  • In the front headlock you control the head, and the body follows.

Common mistakes

  • Snapping with only your arms instead of stepping back.
  • Staying square in front of them, where they can re-shoot on your legs.

Drill it

10 snap-downs each side with a cooperative partner, ending in a locked front headlock.

If it fails, or what comes next

  • Guillotine (arm-in / high elbow) Submission · Front headlock
  • D'Arce choke Submission · Front headlock
  • Go-behind from front headlock Back take · Front headlock

Set it up from

  • Stance, motion & hand fighting
  • Technical stand-up
  • Single-leg defense: whizzer, hip pressure and limp leg
  • Double-leg defense: downblock and sprawl

How to defend it

Front headlock & guillotine defense: Protect your neck with your hands, rebuild your posture, and get your feet under you. Then re-shoot or sit to guard.
