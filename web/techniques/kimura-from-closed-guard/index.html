Kimura from closed guard

A shoulder lock using a figure-four grip on their wrist, turning their arm behind their back. Use it when they post a hand on the mat, often while defending a hip bump.

Closed guard → Submission

Step by step

  1. When they post a hand on the mat, grab that wrist with your same-side hand.
  2. Sit up, reach over their shoulder and under their arm, and grab your own wrist (figure-four grip).
  3. Fall back to the mat and hip out to the side of the arm you're attacking.
  4. Bring your leg over their back to stop them rolling forward.
  5. Rotate their hand toward their back (a paintbrush motion), keeping their elbow at 90°. Go slow.

Details that make it work

  • Elbow bent, hand going behind their back: it's the shoulder that's being attacked.
  • If they grab their own hands to defend, use the kimura grip to sweep or take the back.

Common mistakes

  • Attacking from straight on without hipping out.
  • Cranking fast. Shoulders get hurt before the tap.

Drill it

10 each side, slow finish; partner taps early.

If it fails, or what comes next

Set it up from

  • Closed guard: break posture & control
  • Hip bump sweep
