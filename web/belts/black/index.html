Brown to Black belt

Mastery and refinement. A black belt makes few mistakes, adapts to any body type and style, finishes against people who know the defenses, and can teach the whole art. Many describe black belt as the start of deep learning, not the end.

Typically 1–2 years at brown; about 8–12 years in total. IBJJF minimum: 1 year at brown, age 19+.

What coaches look for

  • You finish your best attacks against people who know the defense
  • You adapt to bigger, smaller, stronger and more flexible partners
  • Very few positions make you panic
  • You can teach a full class and a structured curriculum
  • You understand principles well enough to work out techniques you've never seen
  • You lead on the mat: safety, character and helping others improve

How to get there faster

  • Refine timing and details more than collecting new moves
  • Train with the widest range of partners you can
  • Lead classes and mentor lower belts
  • Keep a beginner's mindset

The 14 techniques

Takedown

  • Arm drag to body lock to mat return

Entry

  • Imanari roll to leg entanglement

Guard

  • Guard retention against heavy pressure: frames and leg pummeling
  • Counter-wrestling from seated guard: front headlock and guillotine

Pass

  • Inside position passing: hands and knees first

Control

  • Power half and spiral ride from turtle

Submission

  • Rear-naked choke against a defended chin
  • Reverse triangle from top (side control or turtle)
  • Mounted guillotine
  • No-gi Ezekiel choke from mount
  • North-south kimura to armbar
  • Buggy choke from under side control
  • Backside 50/50 heel hook

Back take

  • Back attack hand-fight: two-on-one strip to choke or armbar
